Real-astronomy sky sim

Live Sky

A physically-based atmosphere you scrub, fly through, and light-pollute: real weather, sea state, and a full real-sky mode — the Yale Bright Star Catalogue, all 7 planets from NASA JPL orbital elements, all 110 Messier objects, and a continuous Bortle 1–9 slider that fades stars in by real magnitude as the sky darkens.

The interesting part: all 9,096 catalog stars are re-projected every frame from real sidereal time, precession, and atmospheric refraction — not a rotating skybox texture, an actual live ephemeris.

Client-side image/video FX

Image Studio

Seven real-time effect modes — pixel & dither, blur, halftone, edge-ink, CRT, ASCII, and riso — applied to a photo, a video, or your webcam, with region masking (rectangle, brush, or preset zones) and PNG/MP4 export. Nothing ever uploads; every frame is processed on your GPU.

The interesting part: the riso mode models real screen-angle-offset printing (three ink plates at deliberately different halftone angles) — the same physical process that makes riso prints moiré if you get the angles wrong, simulated, not faked with a filter.

Interactive AI explainer

The Descent

How a large language model actually works, top to bottom: seven levels from the chat box down to GPU silicon and back up to a deployed multi-agent system — tokenization, attention, sampling, memory-bandwidth-bound inference, tool-calling, and orchestration. Includes a live "temperature lab" slider that reshapes a token-probability distribution as you drag it.

The interesting part: it frames the KV-cache as a physical constraint, not trivia — why context length directly sets latency, batch-size limits, and cost, as an unavoidable consequence of how transformers read their own weights.

Six bodies, one seam

Metropolis

The whole engine in one place, and six ways to be in it: fly over the harbour, drive its streets, walk at eye level, take the helm of a boat, ride the air as a gull — then bring the gull down onto the water and keep going as a fish. Click any tower and the camera dives through the glass into a warm office interior, the living city still moving outside the window. Scrub the day from pre-dawn to midnight, roll in rain, snow or fog, and switch on a real-astronomy sky for the actual stars above that date.

The interesting part: all six bodies share one movement contract, and each one's character is a law rather than a set of numbers. The car's turning circle grows with the square of its speed — so at full boost it physically cannot make a ninety-degree turn in a city street, and you have to lift off before the junction. The gull is always falling: climbing spends airspeed and diving buys it back, so a hard banked turn costs you altitude. The fish never falls at all, pivots on the spot the way a body with no inertia does, and treats the surface as a ceiling it can break — the breach arc is genuinely ballistic, not an animation.
Also: the sky is a physically-based atmosphere — Rayleigh, Mie and ozone scattering computed into lookup tables every frame — so twilight, the sunrise wavefront and this golden hour fall out of the physics rather than being painted. The people on the sidewalks are skinned characters walking real paths, and the whole thing runs on a phone.
Controls: WASD · Shift to sprint or boost · Space / C to climb and dive · drag to look · scroll to zoom · click a tower to step inside · Esc to leave · six body buttons in the dock. The crossing: as the gull, ride down onto the sea and tap dive — you enter the water as the fish, carrying your speed. On a phone: the bottom half moves and the top half looks, ⤒/⤓ climb and dive, and pushing the stick past its ring is the sprint.

Character playground

The Swing Lab

The room the web-swinging character got built in: lock a target, throw a web at it, ride the arc, and launch off the top — chain webs down the street without touching the ground. Walk to any facade and hold W to spider-crawl straight up it, hands and feet planted on the wall in a diagonal gait, then wall-jump off mid-climb. The lab's own proportions are derived from the swing's pendulum arithmetic; the city level swaps in a full generated skyline with a zombie outbreak spreading through up to 600 civilians on the streets below you.

The interesting part: the city is swingable by construction — ask the generator for 70% of towers to clear the swing's arc-bottom rule and the finished skyline counts exactly 70.0% (252/360). The webs agree: 77% of attaches land on the setbacks, cornices and rooftop plant the silhouette pass built. And the outbreak never reads the player, so the same city falls the same way whether you swing through it or stand and watch.
Controls: LMB lock a target · RMB web it · WASD steer the arc · W/S reel · Space launch off the swing (jump on the ground) · hold W at a wall to crawl up it (S climbs down · Space leaps off) · V camera.

Motocross · trick physics

The Moto Lab

A dirtbike over dunes solved from the bike's own physics — every crest of the shipped terrain (74 of 74) launches at design speed, because the wavelengths are derived from the launch inequality rather than sculpted and hoped about. Hold ↓ in the air to lay it back into a backflip, come round to level, and the landing banks your points: a clean touchdown keeps them all, a botch scrubs them by exactly the speed the crash cost.

The interesting part: the trick scorer never computes a physics number. The landing multiplier is the same float the bike model wrote at touchdown — +154 for the clean ramp backflip, +99 when the same flip over-rotates and the landing keeps 64% of its speed. The HUD can never promise a trick the landing doesn't pay.
Controls: W throttle · S brake · A/D lean-steer · in the air ↓ leans back (hold it for the backflip), ↑ noses down · G the big ramp · V camera · R respawn.

The city marries the terrain

The World Lab

The generated city finally sits on real terrain instead of a flat plinth: a pad is carved flat under every block, the streets between pads become ramps with a hard grade budget, and every tower authors its height off its own pad. Then all three of the engine's bodies run on that one ground: walk the ramped streets, lock + web + swing between towers standing on different pad heights, and press B for the dirtbike — ride a street corridor out over the city's rim and jump the embankment into the wild.

The interesting part: the carve mutates the ONE heightfield the renderer and the physics both read, so nothing can drift — and the swing city's 70% guarantee survives the hills because each tower is now counted against its local street level, not a global zero. Ask for 70% swingable on a hillside and the finished geometry counts exactly 117/168.
Controls: WASD walk · LMB lock a tower · RMB web it · Space jump / release · B mount the dirtbike (W throttle, ↓ leans back) · V camera · R respawn.
